Real Care, Inc. — Documentation Submissions & Compliance
Voiceover Script (English)

Estimated Read Time: ~7 minutes at natural speaking pace

﻿


Welcome to Real Care! This training covers Documentation Submissions and Compliance. Accurate, timely documentation is one of the most important parts of what we do. It ensures compliance with state regulations, supports billing and funding, and protects the health and safety of the individuals we serve. Please listen carefully and take note of the key deadlines and requirements covered here.

First, let's review the provider types you will be working with. Host Home Providers, or HHPs, are non-related caregivers who provide twenty-four-hour care in their own homes and can serve up to three clients at a time. Family Caregivers, or FCGs, are related individuals who provide daily support in the individual's own home. Direct Support Professionals, or DSPs, provide residential services in the individual's or family home on an hourly basis. Respite Providers give temporary relief to primary caregivers. Supported Community Connection Providers, or SCCs, provide community-based hourly services. And Non-Medical Transportation Providers, or NMTs, transport clients to or from day programs or work.

An important concept to understand is Natural Support versus No Natural Support. Natural Supports are unpaid individuals — such as family members, friends, or guardians — who assist with care and supervision. They may help with medication oversight, meals and nutritional support, transportation, and financial management. This distinction matters because it affects documentation requirements. In Family Caregiver settings where Natural Support is present, medication documentation may be completed through Log Notes with Medication Verification. MARs are not required unless specifically directed by an RCI Coordinator or Manager. Meals and nutritional documentation is also not required unless directed by a doctor or coordinator. However, in Family Caregiver settings without Natural Support, the caregiver is fully responsible for all medication administration, meals, transportation, and financial management. This means MARs, Narcotic Records when applicable, and Quarterly Menu Tracking are all required. Important note: Host Home Providers, Respite Providers, Direct Support Professionals, Supported Community Connection Providers, and Non-Medical Transportation Providers do not have Natural Supports. All care responsibilities rest solely with the provider.

Now let's go over submission frequency. There are four categories: monthly, quarterly, as-needed, and annually. The major processing window runs from the first through the tenth of each month, and the attendance and timesheet processing window runs from the twentieth through the twenty-second.

For monthly submissions, most documents are due by the third of each month. Let's walk through each one.

Weekly Log Notes are required for Family Caregivers, Host Home Providers, Direct Support Professionals, and Respite Providers. Log Notes serve as the primary record of weekly service delivery, confirming that authorized services were provided as outlined in the Service Plan. They document health, safety, and behavioral changes that may require follow-up. They support medication oversight when applicable and provide evidence of progress toward ISP goals. This documentation is used for compliance review, audits, and accurate database entry. Log Notes must be legible and complete, dated and signed, cover the full reporting period, be consistent with authorized services and the provider's role, and submitted by the required deadline.

ISP and Goal Tracking is required for Family Caregivers, Host Home Providers, Direct Support Professionals, Respite Providers, and Supported Community Connection Providers. Every individual must have at least one goal, show ongoing weekly progress toward each goal, and show the level of provider support or the individual's independence. Goal tracking may appear on a Goal Tracking Sheet, in Weekly Log Notes, or on a Protocol Tracking Sheet. Real Care personalizes paperwork to best set up caregivers and providers for success.

Daily Receipts of Care are required for all Host Home Providers and Direct Support Professionals unless an approved exception exists. These demonstrate to the state that care is being provided every day, which ensures funding through the daily rate. This may include Protocol Tracking, Rights Modification Tracking, or Residential Tracking. When reviewing these documents, verify that daily receipts are complete, accurate, and submitted on time. Check that daily care is documented consistently, and look for trends or gaps that may require follow-up.

Medication documentation is also due by the third. Depending on the setting, providers submit either MARs and Narcotic Records, Log Notes with Medication Verification, or Log Notes with Self-Med Verification if the individual self-administers medications. Some individuals may have no current prescriptions.

Financial records are due by the third when Real Care is the Representative Payee. This includes the Community Account Record form, which documents all personal spending and deposits, receipts as proof of purchases from Personal Needs Funds, and bank statements to verify account balances. Make sure receipts match the amounts reported on the CAR form and review all documents for completeness.

Attendance records and timesheets have a separate deadline — they are due by the twenty-second of each month. Family Caregivers, Host Home Providers, and Respite Providers submit daily attendance records. Direct Support Professionals and Supported Community Connection Providers submit hourly timesheets. Non-Medical Transportation Providers submit trips, where each trip equals one unit, with a maximum of two trips daily and eight trips weekly. Key monthly deadlines to remember: the eighteenth is when processing begins, the twentieth is the standard processing start, noon on the twenty-second is the first reminder, the twenty-fifth is when processed records and the roster are submitted to Ed, and the first of the following month is the deadline for final corrections and escalation.

For quarterly submissions, Fire and Disaster Drills are required for all individuals and are completed with the Family Caregiver, Host Home Provider, or Direct Support Professional. Menu Tracking is required for Family Caregivers without Natural Support and for Host Home Providers, and also when a medically prescribed diet exists.

For as-needed submissions, Incident Reports must be submitted within twenty-four to forty-eight hours. They are required for all caregivers and providers, and timely reporting is critical. Forward Incident Reports to Real Care staff as soon as possible. Medical Appointment documentation is also submitted as needed — providers are responsible for attendance and documentation.

Annual medical requirements include Medication Orders, PRN and OTC Orders, Adaptive Equipment Checklists when applicable, Physical exams, Dental visits at least twice per year, Vision exams every one to two years, and visits to Specialty Providers such as neurology, psychology, cardiology, or oncology.

Now let's cover the processing workflow and documentation standards. The big picture is: receive and open the email, review attachments, print and prep documents, log in the roster and database, identify issues, communicate with the provider, file and organize, and track follow-ups.

For email processing, fully process one email at a time, starting with the oldest submissions first. If you receive multiple emails from the same provider, process them together. Leave unresolved emails in the Inbox or Pending Issues folder. For printing, use single-sided only, correct orientation, and make sure documents are legible and cropped. Enhance scans with CamScanner if needed. Do not print dark, tiny, or unreadable images, and do not mass print.

When verifying documents, highlight and confirm the client name, provider name, month and year, total days or hours or units, and the provider's signature and date. If a name or date is missing, write it neatly and initial. If a signature is missing, email the provider, use DocuSign if needed, and document it on the roster.

The client database uses color coding: Green means new service, client, or document. Yellow means one to two months non-compliant. Red means two or more months overdue and escalated. White means fully compliant. Log entries before moving to the next email, use clear and complete notes, and use approved acronyms only.

For provider communication, responses must acknowledge what was received, clearly state what is missing, recap all items when multiple emails exist, use approved templates only, and maintain a calm, professional tone. Reminder emails follow a schedule: noon on the twenty-second is the first reminder, noon on the twenty-fifth is the final reminder, and between the twenty-sixth and the first you may send one follow-up maximum. No daily or unscheduled reminders. Escalation triggers include documentation that is thirty or sixty or more days overdue, database entries that don't match documents, provider disputes, repeated errors or refusals, and deadlines at risk.

As a Documentation Support Coordinator, you are the central hub for documentation intake, tracking, and follow-up. Your role ensures compliance, accurate billing, and uninterrupted payments. The focus is on tracking and escalation — not perfection. Your primary goals are to receive and process documentation accurately and on time, prevent billing interruptions and payment delays, maintain compliance with service authorizations and deadlines, and provide clear, supportive communication to providers.

Thank you for completing the Documentation Submissions and Compliance training. If you have any questions, please contact Real Care staff.
